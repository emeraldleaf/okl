# Related work: what the research measured, and where okl stands

Part of okl's documentation; the [README](../README.md) is the short version, and
[Where this sits](WHERE-THIS-SITS.md) covers the product landscape. This page covers the
research literature only. It is dated, it cites arXiv ids, and every figure was read from
the paper's own pages (noted per row), never from a search snippet. Products are not
compared here; a product enters public copy only with a measured comparison and a receipt
in [evals/results/](../evals/results/).

Read on 2026-10-21.

## Seven results from 2026

| Paper | Who | What it shows | Where okl stands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ContextCov**: Bridging the Gap Between Developer Intent and Autonomous Agent Execution. [arXiv 2603.00822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00822), v2 4 May 2026 (pp. 1–3 read) | University of Washington | Compiles instruction files (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `copilot-instructions.md`) into three kinds of executable check: Tree-sitter queries for code patterns, PATH shims that intercept forbidden commands, and architectural validators for layering. On SWE-bench Lite (12 repositories, 300 tasks) it reaches 88.3% constraint compliance against 67.0% prompt-only and 50.3% LLM reflection, at 3.4× lower feedback cost. Code: reSHARMA/ContextCov. | The closest prior art to loading rules as lessons that carry a check. ContextCov generates the checks from the prose and enforces at agent run time; okl keeps the check you wrote beside the lesson that says why, marks the lesson stale when the governed files move, re-runs the check on `okl reverify`, and blocks the push until it passes. The README's "instruction-file linters" bullet describes a narrower category than this; ContextCov is a constraint compiler, and the two are complementary: its generated checks are candidate `--run` values. |
| **TRACE**: Getting Better at Working With You: Compiling User Corrections into Runtime Enforcement for Coding Agents. [arXiv 2606.13174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13174), 11 Jun 2026 (pp. 1–3 read) | University of Notre Dame, IBM Research, Tencent AI Lab | Memory alone does not force compliance: with Mem0 memory, 57.5% of applicable preference checks were still violated. Compiling corrections into runtime checks the agent must pass cut held-out violations from 100.0% to 37.6% in distribution and to 2.0% out of distribution on ClawArena, and from 100.0% to 60.5% on MemoryArena-derived tasks, while matching the strongest memory baseline on task pass. Code: YujunZhou/TRACE_exp and YujunZhou/tellonce. | The same thesis as a lesson with a `--run` check and a gate, measured on different benchmarks. TRACE mines corrections per user at session time; okl's checks are committed, re-run on demand after drift, and gated at push and in CI. Its "access–compliance gap" is the analogue of what okl's harness reports, not the same measure: TRACE counts violations of applicable user-preference checks, while [evals/REPORT.md](../evals/REPORT.md) §5 finding 7 counts reproduction of known defects on seeded tasks, 42% → 7% (78 of 186 unbriefed attempts against 13 of 188 briefed), and claims nothing about general code quality. |
| **Phantom Guardrails**: When Self-Improving Agent Harnesses Fix Failures That Never Happened. [arXiv 2607.13083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13083), 13 Jul 2026 (pp. 1–3 read) | Carnegie Mellon University, Corespeed Inc., Georgia Tech, independent researchers | A suppression-rewarded proposer invented a guardrail for a rule violation that provably never occurred in 15/60 runs when the input carried a harmless rule-like pattern, against 0/60 on featureless input, across five proposer models. Three conditions must co-occur: a rule-shaped pattern, an open-ended rule set, and an instruction that presupposes failures. Under add-only acceptance the phantom is absorbing; warrant-aware acceptance excludes it. Instrument: the Counterfactual Fabrication Lab, deterministic, with a byte-exact oracle. | The failure mode okl's evidence rule exists to prevent: no stamp without a run, `found_by` on every seed record (a corpus convention the CLI does not yet enforce; see the open work below), and "reproduced" in the evals is a blind cross-model judge's verdict against a stated signal, with the receipt committed. The Stop hook's question ("did it surface a lesson worth keeping?") presupposes a lesson, which is their third condition. Their lab is the control condition for any auto-curation experiment. |
| **DreamBench-SWE**: A Multi-Session Memory-Hygiene Benchmark for Software Agents. [arXiv 2608.20664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20664), 21 Aug 2026 (pp. 1–3 read) | Sarthak Singh, independent researcher | Later tasks depend on non-inferable evidence from earlier sessions, graded by hidden executable oracles. The primary contrast between a typed-plus-raw memory probe and verbatim event memory was null (95/180 against 89/180, p=.518). In the successor audit: no memory 21/180, verbatim event memory 82/180, the reference probe 83/180, a hosted Mem0 literal-storage configuration 97/180. In the original v2 fold, the aggregate executable hygiene diagnostics were StaleUseRate 0.200, RepeatedErrorRate 0.571, OverscopeRate 0.167 and IrrelevantImportRate 0.474. | Staleness is measured here as stale *use* by the agent. What no benchmark reports is whether a memory system *detects* that a stored lesson has gone stale before it briefs. okl's drift is such a detector; its precision and recall are the open figure. The paper's own habit of narrowing its claims is the standard this documentation is written to. |
| **Memory Transfer Learning**: How Memories are Transferred Across Domains in Coding Agents. [arXiv 2604.14004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14004), 15 Apr 2026 (pp. 1–3 read) | KAIST, New York University, DeepAuto.ai | A unified memory pool across heterogeneous coding domains improved average scores on 6 benchmarks by 3.7%. The gain comes from meta-knowledge such as validation routines, not task-specific code. Abstraction dictates transferability: Insight-format memories transfer, raw Trajectories cause negative transfer. Transfer scales with pool size and works across models. | A design implication rather than a test of okl: the paper did not evaluate `org` scope, `applies_to` or the Gate type. It argues for recording abstract rules and validation routines, which is what those three exist for, and against ever recording raw traces as lessons. |
| **ReasoningBank**: Scaling Agent Self-Evolving with Reasoning Memory. [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25140), v2 16 Mar 2026 (pp. 1–3 read) | University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Google Cloud AI Research, Yale University | Distils reasoning strategies from successful and failed experiences, judged by the agent itself without ground-truth labels. Its headline maxima, up to 20% relative improvement and up to 16% fewer interaction steps, come from its Table 1 (the WebArena experiment); it also evaluates Mind2Web and SWE-Bench-Verified. Code: google-research/reasoning-bank. | The unverified end of the line: admission by self-judgment. Its released code is the baseline for a verified-against-unverified comparison. |
| **ACE**: Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models. [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618), v3 29 Mar 2026, ICLR 2026 (pp. 1–2 read) | Stanford University, SambaNova Systems, UC Berkeley | Contexts as evolving playbooks updated by generation, reflection and curation. +10.6% on agents (AppWorld) and +8.6% on the finance benchmark, without labeled supervision, using execution feedback. Finds that concise summaries lose domain detail ("brevity bias") and that iterative rewriting erodes contexts ("context collapse"). Code: ace-agent/ace. | The playbook shape okl shares, with admission by reflection rather than by a check that ran. Its brevity-bias finding is a reason to measure the 12-record briefing limit rather than assume it. |

## What the literature leaves open

Of the seven papers reviewed here, none describes a system that admits a lesson only when its
check ran red on the old state and green on the new one in the real repository, okl included: its verify step proves a lesson after
admission, which the third piece of work below would change. None of the benchmarks they use reports a memory
system's precision and recall at detecting that a stored lesson has gone stale. The seven came
from two web searches on 2026-10-20 for memory in coding agents and from those papers' own
related-work sections; this is a reading, not a systematic review, so a claim about the whole
field needs a search this page does not document. Both are measurable with the
existing harness ([evals/ab_harness.py](../evals/ab_harness.py)) and the receipt rules in
[evals/REPORT.md](../evals/REPORT.md).

Three pieces of work follow directly from the rows above and are tracked as issues:

- **Fabrication under the Stop hook** (#251). Phantom Guardrails' third condition is an instruction
  that presupposes failures. The Stop hook asks whether the session surfaced a lesson worth
  keeping; it already accepts "nothing durable" and lists the session's failed commands as
  candidates. Measure how often a session with nothing to learn records a lesson anyway, then
  lead with the evidence question: which check went red and then green, which correction was
  made.
- **Stale-use and over-scope as okl metrics** (#252). DreamBench-SWE's diagnostics have direct
  equivalents: a lesson briefed while its drift was red, and an `org` lesson whose text names
  one repository's paths.
- **Verified against unverified curation** (#253). Run the released ReasoningBank code on the
  harness beside an okl arm that admits a proposed lesson only when its check passes, with
  the Counterfactual Fabrication Lab's featureless pool as the control.
